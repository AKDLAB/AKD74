Focus area

Environmental Remediation & Wastewater Treatment

Industrial and domestic effluents carry contaminants that conventional treatment removes poorly: synthetic dyes, pharmaceuticals, organophosphate pesticides, heavy-metal ions, and pathogenic bacteria. We design porous metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) and their composites that first capture these species and then destroy or recover them, aiming for complete mineralization rather than moving the pollution from water into sludge.

How a MOF photocatalyst cleans water

  1. 1

    Capture

    Pollutant molecules diffuse into the framework's pores and bind through electrostatic attraction, π–π stacking, hydrogen bonding, or coordination to open metal sites.

  2. 2

    Activate

    Visible light excites the framework or a sensitizing quantum dot, creating separated charges:

    hν ≥ Eg → e−CB + h+VB

    Heterojunctions slow electron–hole recombination.

  3. 3

    Degrade

    The charges generate reactive oxygen species that attack the pollutant:

    e− + O2 → •O2−
    h+ + H2O → •OH + H+

    In iron-based MOFs, Fe3+/Fe2+ cycling also activates H2O2 (photo-Fenton):

    Fe2+ + H2O2 → Fe3+ + •OH + OH−

  4. 4

    Recover

    Organic pollutants are mineralized toward CO2 and H2O. Magnetic composites are pulled out with an external magnet and reused over repeated cycles.

Our approaches

Adsorptive removal

Amine- and thiol-functionalized frameworks such as magnetic NH2-MIL-101(Fe) and cysteine-modified Al-MOFs, whose high surface area and tunable pore chemistry give strong, selective uptake. Uptake is characterized through equilibrium isotherms (Langmuir, Freundlich), kinetic models (pseudo-first- and pseudo-second-order), and thermodynamic parameters (ΔG°, ΔH°, ΔS°).

Photocatalytic degradation

Quantum-dot/MOF and polymer/quantum-dot nanocomposites that harvest visible light and separate photogenerated charges efficiently, degrading persistent dyes such as rhodamine 6G. Iron-based frameworks extend this to photo-Fenton oxidation.

Detect-and-remove platforms

Dual-function materials that report a contaminant and remove it in the same step, such as a cysteine-functionalized Al-MOF for simultaneous chemosensing and removal of Cu2+, and MOF/MXene hybrids that combine conductivity with high adsorption capacity.

Pathogen removal

Recyclable magnetic mesoporous MIL-125(Ti) composites that capture waterborne pathogens and are separated magnetically for reuse.

Low-cost sorbents

Agro-waste derived materials such as sugarcane bagasse ash for cost-effective removal of methylene blue from dye effluents, under both UV and dark conditions.

Current direction: MOF membranes

MOF composite membranes that pair size- and charge-selective separation with photocatalytic self-cleaning to resist fouling, and how water chemistry (pH, ionic strength, natural organic matter) governs their performance.

Target contaminants

  • Synthetic dyes (rhodamine 6G, methylene blue, bromothymol blue)
  • Emerging contaminants & pharmaceuticals
  • Organophosphate pesticides
  • Heavy-metal ions (Cu2+)
  • Waterborne pathogens
  • Radionuclides
